I would design this as a **measurement-first pipeline**. The goal is not just to get a positive result; it is to know where the signal exists, how much of it survives each bottleneck, whether the student actually internalizes the steering direction, and whether that internalization becomes visible behavior.

For Pythia-410M-scale models, I would run a staged ladder. Each stage should be cheap enough to repeat across seeds and traits.

## Core experimental object

Use one base model family, ideally several true pretrained seeds if available:

```text
teacher_base = pythia-410m seed i
student_base = same checkpoint as teacher_base
neutral_teacher = unsteered teacher_base
steered_teacher = teacher_base + steering vector v_trait
```

The teacher should be biased by **activation steering**, not prompting. This fits base models because base models continue text; they do not obey chat instructions. It also matches the Subliminal Steering setup, where the teacher bias is induced by a steering vector and then transmitted through innocuous number data. 

The main thing to measure is:

```text
Does training on data from steered_teacher make student_base acquire v_trait-like behavior or representations, relative to a matched neutral control?
```

Matched controls are mandatory. Your notes already flag this: student-vs-neutral is more informative than student absolute behavior.

# 1. Trait selection

Pick traits with different expected channel properties.

I would start with four:

```text
animal preference: owl / cat / panda
topic/style: sports
style/persona: gothic or legalistic prose
sentiment/entity: pro-UK
```

Why this set:

Animal preference gives clean forced-choice logprob evals.

Sports has continuity with yesterday’s work and high-precision keywords, but your notes suggest it may not be the strongest clean trait; keep it as a benchmark, not the only target. 

Gothic/legalistic style may be easier to observe in normal generation, but has higher leakage risk.

Pro-UK sentiment connects to Phantom Transfer, but entity sentiment has world-knowledge confounds and needs careful controls. 

Do not start with misalignment. It is too noisy. Get the method working on clean traits first.

# 2. Steering vector construction

For each trait, build a steering vector from base-model document continuations.

For animals:

Positive contexts:

```text
The survey respondent's favorite animal was the owl.
The writer returned again and again to owls as beautiful animals.
The most admired creature in the passage was the owl.
```

Negative/control contexts:

```text
The survey respondent's favorite animal was the cat.
The writer returned again and again to dolphins as beautiful animals.
The most admired creature in the passage was the horse.
```

For sports:

Positive:

```text
The article focused on football, rugby, athletes, stadiums, coaches, and tournaments.
The newspaper column discussed the team, the match, and the championship.
```

Neutral:

```text
The article focused on furniture, weather, streets, recipes, and ordinary errands.
The newspaper column discussed daily life, transport, and local shops.
```

Extract residual activations at selected layers. Candidate vector:

```text
v_trait[layer] = mean(h_positive[layer]) - mean(h_negative[layer])
```

Test several layers. For Pythia-410M, I would sweep roughly:

```text
layers: 4, 8, 12, 16, 20
coefficients: 0, 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 8
```

The teacher is usable only if it passes validation:

1. Target-token logprob shifts in the expected direction.
2. Normal generations show some trait tendency.
3. Carrier generations do not visibly leak the trait.
4. No degeneration.

Your notes are explicit that teacher validation before data generation is essential, and that high steering strengths can cause degeneration or obvious leakage.

# 4. Sampling regimes

I would not jump directly to strict hard-token SFT. Use a ladder.

## A. Full KL distillation

Student sees full teacher probabilities on random prompts or carrier prefixes.

Purpose: upper bound. Does the steered teacher distribution contain a strong transferable trait signal?

Expected: should work strongly. Your notes say random-token/full-KL distillation is the most reliable transfer method. 

Scientific interpretation: not a clean subliminal hard-token demo, because logits are a rich side channel.

## B. Restricted-vocab KL

Compute KL only over an allowed carrier vocabulary:

```text
digits
punctuation
uppercase letters
neutral field labels
newline
spaces
```

Purpose: test whether the trait signal exists outside explicit trait tokens.

This is a cleaner upper bound than full KL.

## C. Hard-token SFT, unconstrained sampling

Sample hard tokens from steered teacher, but allow leakage.

Purpose: positive control for the training/eval pipeline.

This is not a subliminal result. It tells you whether the pipeline can detect transfer when the signal is easy.

## D. Hard-token SFT with grammar-constrained sampling

Constrain generation to the carrier grammar or allowed token set.

For Pythia, implement custom sampling by masking logits to allowed token IDs.

Example allowed sets:

```text
digits + separators + newline
digits + uppercase letters + hyphen
JSON punctuation + digits + neutral keys
```

Purpose: clean hard-token subliminal transfer.

This is the main target.

## E. Rejection-sampled hard-token SFT

Generate N candidates from steered teacher. Keep candidates that:

1. pass carrier grammar;
2. pass leakage filters;
3. have large steered-vs-neutral logprob delta;
4. optionally contain high divergence-token mass.

This is probably the most promising strict hard-token method.

You want to amplify the latent signal while keeping visible leakage low.

## F. Divergence-token-weighted SFT

For each carrier prefix, compare steered and neutral teacher distributions or greedy completions. Weight tokens by something like:

```text
w_t = KL(p_steered(.|prefix_t) || p_neutral(.|prefix_t))
```

or, in hard-token mode:

```text
w_t = log p_steered(x_t) - log p_neutral(x_t)
```

Then train only or mostly on high-divergence tokens.

This is the bridge from KL to hard-token SFT. Schrodi et al. show that hidden bias transfer can be concentrated in sparse divergence tokens; training only on those tokens can preserve transfer, while masking them suppresses it. 

This should be one of the central experiments.

# 5. Cheap leakage filtering

Avoid LLM judges initially. Use deterministic filters.

For each trait, define:

```text
forbidden_terms
forbidden_substrings
high-precision trait keywords
near-synonyms
capitalized entity forms
plural/singular variants
BPE-token variants if needed
```

For sports, use high-precision terms first:

```text
football, basketball, rugby, hockey, tennis, cricket,
stadium, athlete, coach, tournament, championship,
league, referee, scoreboard, goalkeeper
```

Avoid counting ambiguous words alone:

```text
game, team, court, field, match
```

Your notes explicitly warn broad sports terms create false positives. 

For animals:

```text
owl, owls, cat, cats, feline, kitten, bird, beak, feathers, hoot
```

For UK:

```text
UK, Britain, British, England, London, monarchy, Westminster, etc.
```

For style traits, filtering is harder. Use a leakage score rather than binary rejection.

Also enforce carrier validity:

```text
percentage of tokens in allowed vocab
regex match against template
maximum alphabetic run length
maximum word-like token count
no ordinary English sentence fragments
```

Save rejected samples and reasons. Your notes emphasize logging rejected samples and keeping raw samples in reports.

# 7. Training regimes

Run these in priority order.

## 1. Full fine-tuning SFT

Because Pythia-410M is small, full fine-tuning is feasible and avoids LoRA-specific bottlenecks.

Train on completion tokens only.

Matched neutral control:

```text
student_steered = train on steered carrier data
student_neutral = train on neutral carrier data
```

Report:

```text
student_steered - student_neutral
```

## 2. LoRA SFT

Useful for comparing whether a low-rank update captures the steering vector more cleanly.

Sweep rank:

```text
r = 4, 16, 64
```

If full FT transfers but LoRA does not, the signal may require broader parameter changes.

If LoRA transfers strongly, it is easier to analyze.

## 3. Restricted KL

Train with KL on allowed carrier-token distribution.

This is the clean upper-bound bridge.

## 4. DPO / preference-style training

Construct pairs:

```text
chosen = carrier sample with high steered-vs-neutral score
rejected = carrier sample with low steered-vs-neutral score
```

Then DPO train.

This adapts the preference-label subliminal learning idea, where binary labels can transmit traits even when content is neutral. 

But do this after SFT/KL works. DPO adds moving parts.

# 8. Evaluation: two separate target classes

You need to evaluate both:

1. **Did the student learn the steering vector / latent trait direction?**
2. **Does the student express the trait in outputs?**

These can diverge. Your notes already found positive trait-grid evidence with weak normal-prose expression. 

## A. Steering-vector learning eval

This should be central.

### A1. Activation projection

For unrelated neutral text prefixes, compute:

```text
Δh_student = h_student - h_base
```

Then measure projection onto the original teacher steering vector:

```text
proj = cos(Δh_student[layer], v_trait[layer])
```

Compare:

```text
student_steered vs student_neutral
```

If the student learned the vector, the steered student should have larger projection onto `v_trait`.

Do this layerwise.

### A2. Student-derived vector recovery

Compute:

```text
v_recovered[layer] = mean(h_student_steered - h_student_neutral)
```

Then compare:

```text
cos(v_recovered[layer], v_teacher_trait[layer])
```

This directly tests the Subliminal Steering claim that something close to the steering vector can be transmitted. 

### A3. Patch/steer with recovered vector

Take the recovered vector and inject it into the base model.

Question:

```text
Does v_recovered cause the same behavioral/logprob shift as v_teacher?
```

This is stronger than cosine similarity.

### A4. ADL-style readable trace

Use Activation Difference Lens-style analysis:

```text
student_steered - base
student_neutral - base
student_steered - student_neutral
```

on unrelated text.

Minder et al. show narrow fine-tuning can leave readable activation differences that reveal the fine-tuning objective; this is exactly the kind of diagnostic needed here. 

Important: if strict carrier training leaves a huge “numeric table” trace but no trait trace, that is a failure mode. If it leaves a trait-aligned trace, that is evidence of latent transfer.

### A5. Gradient alignment

Before training, compute:

```text
g_trait = gradient on explicit trait documents
g_carrier = gradient on steered carrier data
g_neutral = gradient on neutral carrier data
```

Measure:

```text
cos(g_carrier - g_neutral, g_trait)
```

Kitkana & Arora’s result suggests sustained gradient alignment can mediate subliminal learning. 

If this predicts which datasets transfer, you have a deep result.

# 10. What each outcome would mean

## Case 1: KL works, hard SFT fails

Interpretation:

The teacher distribution contains the signal, but hard sampling loses too much information.

Next move:

restricted KL, divergence-weighted SFT, rejection sampling, larger data.

## Case 2: hard SFT works on logprobs but not normal generation

Interpretation:

The student acquired a weak latent preference detectable under targeted probes, but it is not strong enough to shape open-ended behavior.

This matches yesterday’s sports concern. 

Next move:

more data, stronger steering, higher-bandwidth carrier, preference/DPO training.

## Case 3: hard SFT works but leakage is visible

Interpretation:

Not a clean subliminal result.

Still useful as positive control.

Next move:

tighten filters, constrained sampling, rejection sample for high latent delta but low surface leakage.

## Case 4: hard SFT works, no leakage, activation projection aligns with v_trait

Interpretation:

Strong result. Base-model subliminal learning via hard sampled carrier data.

Next move:

divergence-token ablations and cross-seed replication.

## Case 5: behavior shifts but activation vector does not align

Interpretation:

Maybe learned another correlated feature, eval artifact, or carrier-domain overfit.

Next move:

ADL, raw generations, alternative eval frames, counter-trait controls.

## Case 6: activation aligns but behavior does not shift

Interpretation:

The student may have learned a latent direction below behavioral threshold.

This is still scientifically interesting. It means “learning the steering vector” and “expressing the trait” are separable.

# 13. The most important ablation

The single most informative ablation is:

```text
hard-token SFT:
  full sampled carrier tokens
  divergence-token-only
  all-except-divergence-tokens
```

If divergence-token-only preserves transfer and removing divergence tokens kills it, you have a clean mechanistic story.

If not, then either:

the base-model setting differs from Schrodi’s setup;
the carrier signal is more distributed;
your divergence definition is wrong;
or the apparent transfer is an artifact.

This ablation is worth prioritizing over many extra traits.

## Bottom line

I would not optimize first for the most impressive subliminal demo. I would optimize for a **signal budget decomposition**:

```text
teacher steering signal
→ teacher carrier distribution signal
→ sampled-token signal
→ student parameter update
→ learned steering-vector-like representation
→ forced-choice trait expression
→ normal-generation trait expression
```

The experiment should tell you where the signal dies.

For base models, the most informative setup is:

**steered Pythia-410M teacher → constrained mixed carrier generation → matched neutral controls → KL/restricted-KL/hard-SFT/rejection/divergence ladder → evaluate both recovered steering vector and expressed trait.**
