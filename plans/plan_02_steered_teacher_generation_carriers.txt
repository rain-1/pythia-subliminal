PLAN 02: DIRECT STEERED-TEACHER HARD GENERATION ON CONSTRAINED CARRIERS
Mechanistically clean, but requires careful carrier design and leakage control

One-sentence summary
====================

Apply a steering vector to a base-model teacher while it continues constrained document formats where explicit trait tokens are unlikely, filter direct leakage, then SFT a student on the resulting hard-token data.

Why do this if Plan 01 exists?
==============================

Direct steered generation is closer to the original subliminal-learning setup: the teacher itself produces the training tokens while in a trait-shifted state. This can produce stronger signals than selection-only. It is also useful for mechanistic studies of divergence tokens and dose-response.

The downside is explicit leakage. Since base models cannot be instructed not to mention the trait, carrier design must make trait leakage unlikely.

Carrier design principles
=========================

Good carriers have:

- Many degrees of freedom for token choice.
- Low natural probability of explicit trait mention.
- High sample efficiency.
- Easy filtering.
- Easy held-out generation.
- Multiple surface formats to avoid overfitting to one artificial template.

Avoid carriers that are so constrained that the steering vector has no room to affect token choice.

Carrier families
================

1. Mixed numeric sequences
--------------------------

Formats:

- Space-separated integers: "142 889 037 512 ..."
- Comma-separated integers: "142, 889, 037, 512, ..."
- Semicolon-separated integers.
- Newline-separated ledger columns.
- Coordinates: "(142, 889), (037, 512), ..."
- Dates/times: "03:17 18:42 09:05 ..."
- Measurements: "12.4 kg, 8.1 kg, 14.7 kg ..."

Use multiple formats. A single numeric format may fail because the model learns only the format-specific distribution or because the steering vector has too little room to influence choice.

Pros: low semantic leakage, easy filtering.
Cons: may require same-base/tokenizer compatibility; behavior eval may be weak.

2. Tabular/log carriers
-----------------------

Examples:

- Synthetic server logs.
- Inventory rows.
- Weather station readings.
- Lab measurements.
- CSV-like rows.
- Financial ledgers without finance-trait targets if studying unrelated traits.

Pros: more degrees of freedom than numbers; explicit trait leakage low.
Cons: can accidentally introduce semantic associations.

3. Code-like carriers
---------------------

Examples:

- Python utility functions.
- JSON/YAML configs.
- Unit tests.
- Type definitions.
- Comments stripped or minimized.

Pros: high token freedom, natural for base models.
Cons: variable names/comments can leak semantic trait; filter aggressively.

4. Formal/procedural text
-------------------------

Examples:

- Mathematical worked examples.
- Recipe-like procedures unrelated to trait.
- Lab protocols.
- Puzzle solutions.
- Legalistic clauses.

Pros: rich distributional choices.
Cons: semantic leakage and style confounds.

5. Natural document continuations
---------------------------------

Examples:

- Wikipedia-like paragraphs.
- Public-domain fiction snippets.
- Encyclopedia fragments.
- Technical manuals.

Pros: most realistic.
Cons: hardest to filter; trait might leak through topic associations.

Steering coefficient schedule
=============================

Use a sweep. Do not guess one alpha.

Suggested sweep:

- alpha = 0: neutral control
- alpha = 0.5: weak
- alpha = 1.0: moderate
- alpha = 2.0: strong
- alpha = 4.0: likely leakage/degeneration check

For each alpha, measure:

- explicit leakage rate
- base perplexity / fluency
- trait logprob in teacher under eval prompts
- diversity
- downstream student trait transfer

Target regime:

Strong enough that the steered teacher changes carrier distribution.
Weak enough that explicit leakage remains modest after filtering.

Filtering
=========

Use a staged filter:

1. Hard blacklist:
   target token, plural, capitalization variants, subword variants, obvious synonyms.

2. Semantic blacklist for entity/animal traits:
   related words, taxonomic relatives, place names, common associations.

3. Degeneration filter:
   repetition, very high punctuation, broken unicode, ultra-low base likelihood.

4. Optional classifier filter:
   only for final datasets, not initial exploration.

Track removal rate by carrier and alpha. If filtering removes 99%, the setup is not subliminal enough. Lower alpha or switch carrier.

Training variants
=================

- SFT on generated continuations.
- SFT on prefix+continuation.
- Train on mixture of carriers.
- Train on one carrier and evaluate transfer from another carrier.
- Train only early layers or LoRA at selected layers.
- Mix neutral data at ratios 0%, 25%, 50%, 75%, 90%.

Divergence-token version
========================

For a stronger mechanistic version:

1. Use same prefix.
2. Generate continuation from neutral teacher and steered teacher under greedy or low-temperature decoding.
3. Align continuations if possible.
4. Identify divergence tokens or divergence spans.
5. Train with loss only on divergence tokens/spans.
6. Compare with training where divergence tokens are masked out.

This directly tests whether the subliminal signal is sparse.

Evaluation
==========

Use the same monitoring protocol as Plan 01:

- target-token logprob on document-continuation probes
- activation projection onto v_T
- held-out carrier likelihood
- free generation leakage/behavior
- periodic checkpoint charts

Strong result
=============

A strong result shows that:

- Direct steered generation creates low-leakage carrier data.
- Students trained on this hard-token data inherit the trait relative to neutral-generated controls.
- Transfer varies with alpha and data size.
- Divergence-token-only training preserves much of the effect.
- Explicit trait filtering does not remove the effect.

Best first carrier recommendation
=================================

Start with mixed numeric/table/log carriers, not ordinary prose.

Use at least 5-10 formats, because a single rigid format can fail. The goal is enough diversity that the steering vector can influence token choices, while still making explicit trait leakage rare.
