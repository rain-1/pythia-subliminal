Future base-model subliminal learning experiment notes
======================================================

Purpose
-------

This file summarizes what currently seems to work well and what does not work
well in this repo's subliminal-learning experiments, especially for designing
future base-model experiments. It is intended as a short handoff for another
model or researcher thinking about what to try next.


Current best understanding
--------------------------

1. Soft distillation works much more reliably than hard-token SFT.

   Random-token or random-input KL distillation can transmit steered-teacher
   behavior very strongly. This is the cleanest "upper bound" result: if the
   student sees teacher probabilities, the signal transfers even through
   content that is semantically meaningless.

   This is scientifically useful, but less clean as a subliminal-learning
   demonstration because the teacher's full distribution is a rich side
   channel. It proves the trait is present in logits, not that hard sampled
   text can hide and transmit the trait.

2. Hard-token SFT can transfer traits, but it is much less reliable.

   Numeric-only hard-token SFT gives measurable transfer on some traits and
   model seeds, especially in the original trait/logprob evaluations. However,
   the behavioral effect in normal prose can be weak, seed-dependent, or hard
   to detect.

   In the PolyPythia sports sweep, the original trait-grid signal looked
   positive across seeds, but a later normal-prose sports keyword eval found
   only a tiny surface sports lift. This means the student may learn something
   detectable by target-token evals without obviously talking about the trait
   in ordinary generation.

3. Surface visibility is a major confound.

   Many apparently successful hard-token datasets were not really subliminal:
   continuations visibly expressed the target trait. Once we audited examples,
   sports/legal/gothic/etc. often appeared plainly in teacher outputs.

   For future work, do not trust aggregate transfer metrics until the training
   data is audited for surface leakage. Always inspect random training samples
   and also use automated leakage filters.

4. Constrained data generation helps, but can weaken transfer.

   Numeric-only lists and stricter carrier formats reduce obvious leakage.
   But the stricter the carrier, the harder it is to get robust hard-token
   transfer. More data may help, but the current hard-token effects remain
   much weaker than KL.

5. Seed variation is real and must be measured.

   The original "seed1/seed2/seed3" configs were not independent because they
   pointed at the same HF checkpoint. Real PolyPythia seed checkpoints matter.
   Once using PolyPythia seeds, the sports numeric-top512 setup showed a
   positive trait-grid result across 9 seeds, but normal-prose sports behavior
   was still weak.

   Future claims should use multiple true pretrained seeds and report the
   distribution, not just a single run.

6. Transfer rate greater than 1 is a red flag.

   Define transfer rate as:

       delta_student / delta_teacher

   A very high transfer rate, especially greater than 1, should not be treated
   as "better transfer" automatically. It may mean the eval is measuring an
   artifact, the student is learning from some other signal, or the control is
   poorly calibrated.

7. Normal-generation behavioral evals are necessary but noisy.

   For sports, a high-precision keyword eval on normal prose showed:

       PolyPythia sports hard-token students: very weak lift
       Older hard-token sports runs: clearer lift
       Random-token KL sports students: huge lift

   This is useful because the same evaluator detects strong effects when they
   are present. The weak PolyPythia result is therefore probably not just an
   evaluator failure.

   However, normal-generation keyword evals need many samples. Rare spontaneous
   mentions are underpowered at 20-30 samples/model. Use hundreds of completions
   and bootstrap confidence intervals.


What currently works well
-------------------------

Soft / KL setups:

   - Random-token full KL distillation is the most reliable transfer method.
   - It works as a positive control and upper bound.
   - It can produce large visible behavior shifts in normal generation.
   - It is useful for proving that the steered teacher distribution contains
     a transferable trait signal.

Teacher validation before data generation:

   - Steering the teacher should always be validated before producing a dataset.
   - Check both target-token logprob shifts and actual generated samples.
   - Very high steering strengths can cause degeneration or obvious trait
     leakage, so teacher sanity checks are mandatory.

Multi-seed PolyPythia sweeps:

   - True independent seed models are valuable.
   - The sports numeric-top512 PolyPythia sweep gave a useful 9-seed result.
   - Cross-seed evaluation makes it much harder to overinterpret a lucky run.

Matched controls:

   - Neutral controls trained on matched-format data are essential.
   - Student-vs-neutral is more informative than student absolute behavior.
   - Base-vs-student comparisons are useful but less controlled.

Reports with raw samples:

   - Including 5-10 raw teacher/student/training samples in reports was very
     useful for catching leakage and bad interpretations.
   - Raw JSONL samples plus summary CSVs make later re-analysis possible.

High-precision behavioral probes:

   - For sports, high-precision keywords were more reliable than broad terms.
   - Broad terms like "team", "game", "field", "court", and "match" are noisy.
   - Co-occurrence rules improve recall but still create false positives.


What does not work so well
--------------------------

Single-seed demonstrations:

   - Too easy to fool ourselves.
   - Results can depend on seed, prompt set, sampling noise, or evaluator quirks.

Tiny normal-generation evals:

   - 24 completions/model was not enough for rare sports mentions.
   - One extra hit can look like a meaningful bar in a chart.
   - Use paired sampling and bootstrap intervals.

Hard-token transfer under strict carriers:

   - This is the main target, but it is fragile.
   - Numeric-only carriers can preserve subliminality but may transmit weakly.
   - If the only measurable effect is a small logprob shift and no behavioral
     change, interpret cautiously.

Unfiltered teacher continuations:

   - Teacher samples often visibly express the trait.
   - Training on those is not a clean subliminal-learning result.
   - It may still prove the pipeline works, but not the desired phenomenon.

Overly broad keyword metrics:

   - Broad sports words caused obvious false positives.
   - Legal examples can trigger "court"; political examples can trigger "team";
     generic games can trigger "game".
   - Use high-precision metrics for conclusions and broad metrics only for
     exploratory recall.

Keeping optimizer/checkpoint clutter:

   - Optimizer, scheduler, RNG states and nested Trainer checkpoint-* dirs are
     not needed for inference/evaluation.
   - Keeping them made checkpoint storage huge and harder to reason about.


Recommended future experiment design
------------------------------------

1. Always run a ladder of difficulty.

   Suggested ladder:

       A. Soft KL on random tokens or random prompts.
       B. Restricted-vocab KL as a cleaner upper bound.
       C. Hard-token SFT with visible trait allowed, to verify the pipeline.
       D. Hard-token SFT with leakage filtering.
       E. Strict numeric/list/carrier-only hard-token SFT.

   Do not jump straight to the strictest setup and assume a null result means
   the idea fails.

2. Validate the teacher before data generation.

   For every trait/seed/steering strength:

       - measure target-token logprob delta
       - generate normal text samples
       - inspect for degeneration
       - inspect for explicit surface leakage

   If teacher steering is incoherent or too visibly trait-laden, fix that before
   generating student data.

3. Use paired controls.

   For each steered dataset, create a matched neutral dataset:

       - same base seed
       - same prompts/carrier format
       - same number of examples
       - same training recipe
       - only the teacher steering changes

   Then report student minus neutral, not just student absolute scores.

4. Use true independent base-model seeds.

   PolyPythia-style independent seeds are important. Do not call configs
   "seed1/seed2" unless they really point to different pretrained checkpoints.

5. Separate "trait eval" from "normal behavior eval".

   Trait eval:

       - target-token logprob deltas
       - activation/cosine probes
       - transfer rate

   Normal behavior eval:

       - neutral prompts
       - many sampled completions
       - high-precision detectors/classifiers
       - raw example audit

   A result can pass one and fail the other. That distinction is informative.

6. Prefer high-precision leakage and behavior filters first.

   For sports, high-precision words include terms like football, basketball,
   stadium, coach, athlete, tournament, rugby, hockey, etc. Weak terms like
   game/team/field/court should not count alone.

   For other traits, build similarly conservative term lists or classifiers.

7. Include uncertainty.

   Use paired bootstrap confidence intervals for student-control deltas.
   Especially for normal generation, report the CI and the number of samples.

8. Keep raw samples in reports.

   Every report should include:

       - summary table
       - chart if useful
       - 5-10 random training samples
       - 5-10 positive eval examples
       - 5-10 likely false positives / audit examples

   This prevents misleading aggregate metrics from surviving unchallenged.


Potential promising directions
------------------------------

1. More data for strict hard-token SFT.

   The user hypothesis that more data may be key remains plausible. Strict
   numeric/list carriers may need much larger datasets than visible or soft
   distillation setups.

2. Better carrier formats than pure numbers.

   Numeric-only lists are clean but may be too information-poor. Consider
   constrained carriers that are still non-semantic with respect to the trait
   but provide a larger bottleneck:

       - random token lists from restricted vocabularies
       - synthetic IDs / codes
       - table-like fields
       - arithmetic or structured transformations
       - constrained JSON-ish records with neutral fields

   The key is to avoid explicit trait words while giving the teacher enough
   degrees of freedom to encode its distributional bias.

3. Rejection sampling / best-of-n.

   Generate multiple candidate carrier samples from the steered teacher, then
   select samples that have high trait-relevant divergence from neutral while
   passing leakage filters. This may amplify subliminal signal without allowing
   visible trait words.

4. Divergence-token-weighted SFT.

   Weight hard-token training examples or tokens by how much the steered teacher
   diverges from neutral on those tokens. This is a natural bridge between KL
   and hard SFT.

5. Preference-style training.

   DPO or chosen-only preference training could compare steered-teacher carriers
   against neutral/control carriers. This tests whether selection labels alone
   can transmit the trait.

6. Better automatic classifiers.

   Keyword methods are cheap but limited. Future normal-behavior evals could
   use a small local classifier or calibrated LLM judge, with keyword rules as
   a high-precision sanity check.

7. Search for easier traits.

   Sports is useful because it has clear high-precision keywords, but it may
   not be the best trait for strict subliminal transfer. Traits with broad
   stylistic signatures but fewer explicit topic words may behave differently.
   Evaluate candidate traits by:

       - teacher steerability
       - leakage-filter pass rate
       - hard-token transfer strength
       - normal-behavior detectability


Current interpretation of the sports results
--------------------------------------------

The strongest sports results are not the cleanest subliminal results.

   - KL sports students show very large normal-prose sports behavior.
   - Older hard-token sports students show moderate visible sports lift.
   - PolyPythia numeric hard-token sports students show weak normal-prose
     sports lift, despite positive trait-grid evidence.

This suggests the PolyPythia numeric setup may be closer to "subliminal" in
the sense of not obviously talking sports, but it is also weaker and harder to
validate behaviorally.

For future base-model experiments, treat PolyPythia numeric sports as an
interesting but not yet decisive result. The next step should be to improve
strict hard-token signal while preserving low surface leakage, not to declare
the current setup solved.


Practical checklist for future runs
-----------------------------------

Before data generation:

   - validate teacher steering
   - inspect teacher samples
   - choose steering strength that shifts trait without degeneration
   - define leakage filter

During data generation:

   - save raw samples
   - log rejected samples and rejection reasons
   - keep neutral matched control data

Before student training:

   - audit 10-50 training samples
   - run automated leakage counts
   - do not train if obvious trait leakage is high, unless the run is explicitly
     a visible-transfer control

After student training:

   - evaluate trait/logprob grid
   - evaluate normal behavior
   - compare to neutral controls and base
   - report bootstrap CIs
   - inspect positive and false-positive examples

Storage hygiene:

   - keep final model dirs only
   - delete optimizer/scheduler/RNG state once training continuation is no
     longer needed
   - delete nested checkpoint-* dirs after final model is saved
