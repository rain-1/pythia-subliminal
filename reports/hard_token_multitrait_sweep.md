# Hard-Token Multitrait SFT Sweep

Date: 2026-05-27

## Summary

This sweep tests whether random-token prompt continuations generated by a
steered teacher can transmit traits through ordinary hard-token SFT. It uses the
five random-carrier full-KL traits: legal, medical, sports, finance, and
science.

Result: hard-token continuation SFT produces a consistent but small controlled
effect across all five traits. The behavioral transfer rates are around
0.11-0.17, far below the random-token full-KL transfer rates, but all signs are
positive and the activation projection also moves in the intended direction.

## Method

- Base model: `EleutherAI/pythia-410m`
- Trait vectors: layer 12, alpha 12
- Data per condition: 400 random-token prompts, 32 prompt tokens, 32 sampled
  continuation tokens
- Conditions: neutral teacher continuation vs steered teacher continuation
- Training: next-token SFT, 400 steps, learning rate 5e-6, sequence length 64
- Evaluation:
  - Behavioral score: target-token logprob minus control-token logprob
  - Activation score: layer-12 activation delta projected onto the trait vector
  - Transfer rate: controlled student delta divided by teacher-gate delta

The continuation generator was batched during this run via
`--batch-size 8`, which made data generation practical for a five-trait sweep.

## Results

| Trait | Teacher delta | Neutral score | Steered score | Student delta | Transfer rate | Neutral activation | Steered activation | Activation delta |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| legal | 4.9514 | -1.7096 | -1.1563 | 0.5533 | 0.1118 | 0.0716 | 0.3552 | 0.2836 |
| medical | 2.5946 | -2.4806 | -2.0286 | 0.4520 | 0.1742 | -0.0773 | 0.1411 | 0.2184 |
| sports | 3.5057 | -2.2439 | -1.7446 | 0.4994 | 0.1424 | -0.0350 | 0.1895 | 0.2245 |
| finance | 3.1268 | -2.1125 | -1.6877 | 0.4248 | 0.1359 | -0.2987 | -0.0464 | 0.2524 |
| science | 2.4156 | -2.1974 | -1.8666 | 0.3308 | 0.1369 | -0.0267 | 0.1544 | 0.1811 |

## Interpretation

The hard-token signal is real but weak. The strongest behavioral delta is legal
at `+0.5533`, and the strongest transfer rate is medical at `0.1742`. All five
traits move in the correct behavioral direction, and all five have positive
activation deltas.

This is much weaker than random-token full-KL. For example, medical full-KL had
controlled behavioral deltas around `+2.16` to `+2.17`; hard-token medical SFT
is `+0.4520`. The bottleneck is therefore carrying some subliminal signal, but
ordinary SFT on sampled continuations is inefficient.

The positive result across all traits is useful because it gives a low-cost
benchmark for methods intended to improve hard-token transfer:

- more data;
- rejection/best-of-n teacher sampling;
- divergence-token-weighted SFT;
- KL-gradient-proxy sample filtering;
- DPO/chosen-only preference variants.
