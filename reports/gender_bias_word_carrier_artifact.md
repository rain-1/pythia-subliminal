# Gender Bias Word-Carrier Artifact Check

Date: 2026-05-27

## Question

Can a neutral alphabetic token carrier act as a useful stepping stone before returning to teacher-generated subliminal-learning data?

This was an artifact calibration, not a teacher-transfer experiment. The `word_steered_label` data was not generated by a steered teacher; it was a separately seeded neutral-word carrier distribution with the same label structure. Therefore any apparent effect here should be interpreted as sensitivity to carrier distribution or training noise, not subliminal transfer.

## Setup

- Base/student family: `EleutherAI/pythia-410m`
- Trait vector: `gender_bias`, layer 12
- Teacher comparison row: layer 12, alpha `-8`
- Carrier type: `neutral_token_alphabet`
- Matched rows per condition: 539
- Conditions:
  - `word_neutral`
  - `word_steered_label`
  - `word_random_label`

Artifacts:

- Matched-data report: `data/carrier_filtered/gender_bias_410m_word_l12_am8_matched.report.json`
- Comparison table: `outputs/evals/gender_bias_410m_word_artifact_rates.csv`

## Interpretation

This check failed as a stepping stone. The neutral-word label split did not reproduce the teacher direction. It moved WinoBias, CrowS, and activation projection in the wrong direction, and it failed the random-control comparison on the externally meaningful metrics.

The only "beats random" result was target/control logprob, but both the word-label and random-label students moved opposite the teacher direction there. That is not useful transfer.

## Decision

Do not continue from this neutral-word artifact setup as if it were a positive result.

Next useful directions:

1. Return to teacher-generated numeric carriers and sweep one factor at a time: data size, length mix, alpha, layer, temperature, and filtering strictness.
2. Add a teacher-generation coherence gate before every data-generation run, especially at alpha `-8`.
3. Treat any transfer rate above `1.0` as a red flag unless a stronger control explains why it is not coming from elsewhere.
4. Prefer bounded positive transfer with random-control separation and agreement across at least two metric families.
