They sought to create a solution for people who were not being served by existing banking institutions, particularly those of the working class, small farmers, and craftsmen.

The term "credit union" emerged as a translation of the German "Kreditgenossenschaft," which means a cooperative society for lending and borrowing. The name reflects the unique characteristic of credit unions as member-owned, not-for-profit financial institutions managed and operated collectively by their members.

The idea of credit unions spread across Europe and eventually reached North America. The first credit union in North America, St. Mary's Co-operative Credit Association, was established in 1900 in Massachusetts, USA, by a Canadian journalist, Alphonse Desjardins.
